# What should buzz, and how often — 2026-09-10

Two questions the codebase had never asked, prompted by the owner testing on a
real device and reporting it "buzzing all over the place."

They turn out to be independent, and only one of them is a defect.

1. **Dose** — *how often does the wrist buzz?* Two defects, both fixed and
   shipped in this batch.
2. **Rubric** — *what should buzz at all?* A measurement, not a fix. It says
   the current design has a ceiling that no threshold can lift, and the way
   past it is an architectural choice the owner should make.

---

## 1. Dose — two defects

Every haptic test in this repo checked that the *right* cue plays. None had
ever counted how **often**. Both defects below were invisible to the whole
suite and to the file-replay report, because the report counts *escalations* —
the moment a level changes — and the wrist also buzzes on schedules the report
does not model.

### 1a. The proportional pulse train — `14.5–36 buzzes per minute`

`PulseEngine` emits one tap per 1 s audio window while the wearer is ≥ 6 dB
over their own baseline. Measured by porting `SentinelDetector` + `PulseEngine`
exactly (`scripts/pulse_dose.py`) over the two committed real recordings:

| recording | what it is | dose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `family_real` | father and son, one mildly raised turn | **14.5 buzzes/min** |
| `poker6_real` | a poker game — nobody is arguing | **36.0 buzzes/min** |

Two details make it worse than the rate suggests:

- The baseline **deliberately excludes** over-threshold windows, so sustained
  loudness cannot drag the bar up and self-suppress. Correct for *detection*,
  but it means the longer the argument the longer it buzzes, without end.
- The "Pulse speed" preference (0.25 / 0.5 / 1 s) is **inert**. Every option is
  ≤ the 1 s window, so the spacing clamp never binds and all three deliver an
  identical dose. A wearer who felt nagged and slowed it down would have felt
  no difference at all.

**Fixed:** default `off`. Not deleted — as a live self-monitoring gauge it does
its job; it is simply not a coaching default. An unrecognised/corrupt pref now
also reads as off rather than falling back to the densest setting the app has.
Pinned by `PulseDoseTest`.

### 1b. The PRD §6 reminder — `18 buzzes in three minutes, forever`

Level 3 repeated every 10 s while the level held, and `NudgePolicy`
de-escalates only one level per 20 s of quiet. So a sustained argument — the
exact situation level 3 exists for — buzzed every ten seconds and never
stopped.

**Fixed:** `NudgeHapticSchedule.reminderIntervalMs` doubles the gap per repeat,
capped at level 1's two-minute cadence. Level 3 still speaks up promptly
(10 s, then 20, 40, 80) and then settles to a gentle pulse rather than falling
silent — the "never feels like the watch gave up" rule is preserved. **Three
minutes now costs 5 buzzes instead of 18.** A *new* escalation resets the
back-off, so a situation that genuinely worsens is still reported promptly.
Pinned by `ReminderDoseTest`.

## 2. Rubric — what *should* fire

### Why our own fixtures were the wrong yardstick

The three TTS scenes were generated by telling a speech model to sound angry.
It complied through **wording and pacing, not amplitude**. Their "heated" turns
measure **+0.0 to +1.8 dB** over the speaker's own median — they cannot reach
the first rung (+6 dB) at all, and in `scene_couple_escalation` the loudest
"you" turn is the **calm opening line**.

So the acoustic lane had effectively never been validated on anything except
~30 seconds of the owner's family recording. The scenes remain valid for the
*text* lane; they are close to worthless for the *acoustic* one.

### The calibration set

`scripts/heat_rubric.py` — every candidate signal beside a corpus's own emotion
label, features computed with `server/prosody.py`'s own frame estimator (the
one the phone ports), so a threshold chosen here transfers to the device rather
than to a lookalike.

| corpus | speakers | why this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CREMA-D | 91 | the **same sentences** neutral *and* angry, so "louder than your own baseline" is measured per speaker rather than approximated; plus LO/MD/HI intensity |
| RAVDESS | 24 | explicit normal/strong intensity on **every** emotion |

Negatives are chosen as deliberately as positives. **HAPPY** is a hard negative
because excited speech is loud and buzzing someone for laughing is a loudness
detector's most embarrassing failure. RAVDESS's **calm** is a negative because
a detector that fires on deliberately relaxed speech is broken in the most
obvious way available. **SAD** and **FEAR** are negatives because a coach that
nudges someone for being upset is the opposite of the product.

Audio lives in `tmp/` and is never committed (CREMA-D ODbL, RAVDESS
CC BY-NC-SA).

### Result

CREMA-D, 7,442 clips, dB over each speaker's own neutral:

| rung | catches angry | fires on non-angry | fires on HAPPY |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| +6 (level 1) | 84.0% | 18.2% | **44.5%** |
| +10 (level 2) | 61.3% | 7.6% | — |
| +14 (level 3) | 34.8% | 2.9% | — |

RAVDESS agrees and is worse: +6 dB catches 89.1% of angry and fires on **40.8%
of all non-angry clips, 66.1% of happy ones**.

**Loudness is not broken.** As an arousal detector it is excellent — AUC 0.972
against neutral speech, and it tracks the corpus's own anger intensity
monotonically (low +6.2 dB, medium +11.7, high +19.0). The defect is specific:

```
AUC angry vs NEUTRAL   0.972
AUC angry vs HAPPY     0.799
```

Anger and joy are both high-arousal. Loudness measures arousal. **The wrist
cannot distinguish shouting at your wife from laughing with your friends**, and
no threshold repairs it — the sweep from +2 to +14 dB trades recall against
false alarms without ever separating the two.

### The dimension we were throwing away

`tone_id`'s default backend returns **arousal, dominance and valence**. Every
caller has only ever read `arousal`.

> The 2026-09-08 rejection of audio tone tested escalation-*delta*, an
> arousal-derived quantity. It never tested valence. That was a gap in that
> evaluation, not a verdict on this.

Out of fold, grouped by speaker so no speaker appears in both train and test:

| feature set | AUC all | AUC vs HAPPY |
| --- | --- | --- |
| loudness alone (what ships) | 0.905 | 0.776 |
| arousal alone | 0.879 | 0.713 |
| valence alone | 0.602 | **0.801** |
| loudness + valence | **0.920** | **0.823** |
| loudness + valence + dominance | 0.921 | 0.823 |

At matched recall the false alarms roughly halve:

| detector | catches angry | fires on HAPPY |
| --- | --- | --- |
| loudness alone, +6 dB | 83.2% | 46.4% |
| loudness + valence | 81.6% | **31.6%** |
| loudness + valence, stricter | 74.4% | **21.2%** |

**Better, not solved.** Audio alone still buzzes a fifth to a third of happy
speech.

## What this implies — for the owner to choose

The remaining signal has to be the **words**. "You never listen to me" and
"that's amazing" are acoustically similar and semantically opposite. The phone
already has that (the LLM's text tone, relayed to the watch as
`aggressive_tone`); the watch's **own mic path nudges on loudness alone**.

Three options, not mutually exclusive:

- **A. The wrist stops nudging on loudness alone** — require the server's text
  tone to confirm before channel A escalates from the watch's own mic. Biggest
  quality win; costs latency (the instant tier's whole point is firing ~1 s
  after a turn, before the words arrive) and fails offline.
- **B. Add valence as a gate** — cheap-ish, no text needed, halves the happy
  false alarms, keeps the instant tier. Needs `tone_id` on the server and a
  decision about on-device cost.
- **C. Raise the ladder** — smallest change, but the data says it only trades
  recall for false alarms and never fixes the happy case. Also a
  cross-language contract change (`nudge_policy.json`), so the blast radius is
  large for the benefit.

Recommendation: **B now, A as the real fix.** C alone is not worth the contract
churn.

Deliberately **not** done unilaterally overnight: these change *what fires*,
which is a product decision on one night's new evidence. The two dose defects
above were unambiguous and are shipped.

```bash
python scripts/pulse_dose.py                      # the dose arithmetic

```
