4. BAD Cloud teacher capability and rights gaps
Bad — Cloud teacher capability and rights gaps. Use the research results to target the next training round; do not deploy this candidate.

A stronger audio teacher is worth a controlled comparison, but being multimodal does not automatically mean accepting raw audio. Official model pages checked November 5, 2026 list audio as unsupported for GPT-6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Astra. They support text and images. Codex model choice alone does not give us a raw-audio labelling interface.

OpenAI’s audio guide demonstrates native audio input using gpt-audio-1.5. That is a plausible cloud comparison candidate, not a measured winner. A transcript-only model misses vocal delivery; using it would answer a different question.

Recommended experiment: first validate the audio model on existing human-rated development clips, hiding actor labels, human votes and filenames that reveal the answer. Compare per-emotion errors and confidence with local candidates, then confirm on fresh human-rated natural speech. Keep machine labels explicitly marked and separate from human ground truth; review disagreements and low-confidence cases. Only expand the training library after a teacher passes this evaluation. No cloud inference or new labelling run was performed for this update.

Commercial training rights require a separate check. The OpenAI Services Agreement, §3.3 and “Permitted Exception”, restricts using outputs to develop competing models; its classifier exception excludes models distributed or made commercially available to third parties. This does not determine whether your specific app is competing, but we cannot assume permission to train a shipped phone model from those outputs. Confirm applicable contractual rights before bulk teacher labelling, or use a teacher whose terms expressly permit this use. A paid cloud benchmark and a free-to-run phone model are separate cost decisions.
