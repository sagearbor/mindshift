4. GOOD Agreement-only training compared
Good — Agreement-only training compared. Nothing to do.

We tested your idea directly: train on 1,849 clips where actor and unique listener winner agree, versus all 4,489 training clips using full listener distributions. Both use exactly the same broad test. This comparison changes both sample count and label mixture, so it does not isolate “nuance” as a causal mechanism.

tiny53.9%57.1%-3.2 points

Agreement-only training scored higher in 3 of the three held-out comparisons. This is a useful signal in favor of your proposed filter, while the opposite development ranking shows that actor sampling matters.

Positive differences favor all-vote training; negative differences favor agreement-only training. Development favored all-vote training for all three encoders, but this does not guarantee the same direction on independent actors. Treat these as descriptive comparisons, not proof of a universal winning cohort. Recommendation: retain all labels and full listener distributions; keep agreement-only training as a supported option and comparison, rather than discarding ambiguous data permanently.

6. BAD Automatic labeller failed validation
Bad — Automatic labeller failed validation. Use the research results to target the next training round; do not deploy this candidate.

Local Qwen2.5-Omni-7B audio development macro F1 was 38.8%; transcript-only was 12.6%. These are development scores, not held-out test claims. Neither qualifies to manufacture trusted training labels.

No large machine-labelled pool was created or trained on. Admission checks exist for quality, licences, provenance, speaker/audio overlap, model revision and finite confidence values. A mass collection/labelling job is intentionally not implemented. For the next round, obtain independent human audio-only ratings for licensed natural conversational clips, then test a stronger teacher against them before scaling.
